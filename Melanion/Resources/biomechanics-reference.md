# Running Biomechanics & Training Reference

## Pace Zones
- Easy / recovery: >5:30/km
- Aerobic base: 5:00–5:30/km
- Tempo: 4:15–4:59/km
- Threshold: 3:50–4:14/km
- VO2max interval: 3:30–3:49/km
- Speed: <3:30/km

## Heart Rate Zones (% of max HR)
- Zone 1 — Recovery: <60%
- Zone 2 — Aerobic base: 60–70%
- Zone 3 — Tempo: 70–80%
- Zone 4 — Threshold: 80–90%
- Zone 5 — VO2max: >90%

## Key Running Form Metrics
- **Cadence**: 170–180 spm optimal for most runners; below 160 increases injury risk
- **Ground contact time**: <250 ms efficient; >280 ms indicates energy loss
- **Vertical oscillation**: 6–8 cm optimal; higher = wasted energy
- **Stride length**: increases naturally with speed; do not force it
- **Running power**: useful for comparing effort across varied terrain

## Grade-Adjusted Pace (GAP)
GAP normalises pace for elevation: +8 s/km per 1% uphill grade, −5 s/km per 1% downhill grade. Use GAP to compare efforts across hilly and flat courses.

## Recovery Metrics
- **HRV (ms)**: Higher = better recovery. Drop of >20% from personal baseline = caution
- **Resting HR**: Elevation of >5 bpm above baseline = incomplete recovery
- **VO2 max (ml/kg/min)**: Elite male runner: >65; recreational: 45–55
- **Sleep**: 7–9 hours linked to best performance; <6 hours impairs glycogen resynthesis
- **HR recovery (1-min)**: >20 bpm drop = good cardiac fitness; <12 bpm = concern

## Pacing Strategy
- **Negative split**: second half faster — optimal race strategy for most distances
- **Positive split**: second half slower — common in races where athletes go out too hard
- **Even split**: consistent pace throughout — good for training runs

## Common Training Principles
- 80/20 rule: ~80% of weekly volume at easy/aerobic pace, ~20% at threshold or above
- Long run: should not exceed 30% of weekly volume
- Step-back weeks: reduce volume by ~20% every 3–4 weeks for recovery adaptation
- Tapering: reduce volume 20–40% in final 2–3 weeks before a target race
